Design notes

Built to be photographed

Every showpiece element in your collection is on this track at once: the 4-piece ramp bridge throws the racing line 93 mm over its own back straight at a 60° crossing angle, and all six high-banked R3 pieces are deployed as three wall-angled sweepers — one feeding the cascade after the bridge, two back-to-back in the finale that launches cars up the ramp.

The silhouette is an A-frame figure-eight: a peaked left lobe climbing to the top of the layout, a compact banked teardrop on the right, and the cream-colored deck slicing across the middle. From the couch it reads as three layers of track — banked wall, flat canyon, flying bridge.

It is also a genuinely good race track: the pit straight packs the Control Unit, Wireless+ charging zone and a double lane change; the underpass straight hides a second double lane change; and the out-to-in lane-change curve sets up a corner-entry pass right before the banked finale.

Exact closure, banked and all. The banked pieces appear as separated 60° corners rather than one 180° wall — a continuous banked half-loop provably cannot close against R1 curves (its 1386 mm offset is a third of a piece out of step with everything else). Split into three apexes, the solver closes this layout to 10-12 mm.

Bonus fairness. Because it crosses itself, net turning is 0° and both slots measure an identical 11 876 mm — same equal-lanes property as The Equalizer, in a completely different shape.
Deck logistics. The underpass runs 53 mm from the crest joint — maximum clearance. Keep the bridge feet clear of the banked finale's outer shoulder, and fit the border end-pieces on the cascade exits.
