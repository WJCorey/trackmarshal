Design notes

Every usable piece, one exact hexagon

This layout puts your entire drivable collection on the floor at once — all 16 standard straights, both 1/3 and both 1/4 straights, every lane changer, the Control Unit, the charging straight, all 11 R1 curve pieces, all 6 banked curves and the full 4-piece ramp kit as an airborne hump. Total: 16.0 m of racing line, roughly double the starter-set oval.

The shape is an equiangular hexagon whose corners alternate banked R3 sweepers with flat R1 corners. That alternation is not styling — it is the closure proof. Each 60° corner behaves like two tangent stubs of length r/√3; because every side sits between one banked (693 mm) and one flat (297 mm) corner, all six sides get the same tangent sum and the hexagon closes if the sides satisfy two simple length equations:

B + C = E + F and A − D = E − B

with A=2070, B=1725, C=1236.25, D=2415, E=1380, F=1581.25 mm. The solver confirms closure to 0.000 mm.

Bus stops that cancel. The two chicane jogs on the front straight and the two on the back straight advance the car by 297·√3 mm each — an irrational number that can never be trimmed away with straight pieces. They are placed two-per-straight in opposite directions so the √3 terms cancel exactly.

The hump is real airtime. The 4 ramp pieces (concave–convex–convex–concave) form an up-and-over crest on the climb side. Support the crest joint per the 20587 kit instructions and brake before Turn 2.
Banked caveat. The catalog marks banked R3 pieces solverReady:false — plan-view geometry uses the flat-R3 approximation, so expect to absorb a few mm of joint flex around the three banked apexes. Everything else is exact.
